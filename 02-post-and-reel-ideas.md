# Post and Reel Ideas (DRAFT)

**Approach: Positive Discipline.** Every idea should model kind *and* firm, avoid punishment, shame and bribes, and leave parents feeling encouraged rather than blamed.

Format notes: Reels are 9:16 with on-screen text, since most people watch without sound. Carousels are 1080×1350 (4:5). Put the hook in the first 1–2 seconds of a Reel and on slide 1 of a carousel.

---

## Week 1 (full outlines)

### Mon Reel: "A misbehaving child is a discouraged child"
- **Hook (on screen, 0–2s):** "Your child isn't trying to make you mad. They're trying to belong."
- **Beat 2 (2–12s):** Talking to camera: "In Positive Discipline we say a misbehaving child is a discouraged child. That idea comes from psychiatrist Rudolf Dreikurs. Every child wants to feel they belong and that they matter."
- **Beat 3 (12–22s):** "When they don't feel that, they try to get it in mistaken ways, like constant attention-seeking or power struggles."
- **Beat 4 (22–32s):** "So instead of asking 'How do I stop this?', ask 'What is my child really trying to tell me?' Connection first, then correction."
- **Closing text:** "Follow for more Positive Discipline 💛"
- **Visual idea:** You at a kitchen table, natural light. Optional B-roll of toys or a messy floor.

### Wed Carousel: "Kind AND firm: 6 phrases"
1. **Cover:** You can be kind and firm at the same time. Here's how 👇
2. "I love you, and the answer is no."
3. "You're upset, and it's still time to leave the park."
4. "I won't let you hit. You can stomp your feet instead."
5. "What's your plan for getting your homework done?" *(Curiosity question)*
6. "I'll be ready to read when your pajamas are on." *(Decide what YOU will do)*
7. "Let's find a solution that works for both of us."
8. **CTA slide:** Kind = respect for your child. Firm = respect for yourself and the situation. Save this 💛

### Fri Reel: "Mistakes are opportunities to learn"
- **Hook:** "I'm a certified Positive Discipline educator, and yes, I still lose my cool sometimes."
- **Body:** In Positive Discipline, mistakes are wonderful opportunities to learn, for parents too. Try the 3 Rs of Recovery:
  1. **Recognize:** "I yelled."
  2. **Reconcile:** "I'm sorry. That wasn't respectful."
  3. **Resolve:** "Let's figure out together what we can do next time."
- **Point:** Kids learn far more from watching you repair than from watching you be perfect.
- **Closing text:** "No perfect parents, just ones who repair 💛"
- *(If you'd rather not share personal stories, frame it as "what I tell every parent I work with.")*
